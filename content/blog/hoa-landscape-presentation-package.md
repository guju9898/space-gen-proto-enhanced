HOA and design-review submissions have a particular kind of friction.

You have done the design work. The client is ready. Now the project has to survive a review by people who may never have visited the site — who will decide based on whatever is in the submission packet, often under time pressure, alongside a stack of other requests.

If the packet is vague, the outcome is often delay: tabled for more information, sent back for revisions, or deferred to the next meeting cycle. That is not always a rejection of the design. Sometimes it is a response to a presentation that did not give reviewers enough to evaluate clearly.

A strong presentation package does not control how a board votes. It gives reviewers a clear basis for discussion — so they can understand what is being proposed on the actual property, in context, with enough detail to ask informed questions.

## What belongs in an HOA-facing landscape package

Requirements vary by association, city, and architectural review committee. Always confirm the checklist your specific HOA publishes before you submit. The following items appear frequently in landscape and hardscape design-review requests:

### Property context

- **Site overview** — showing the lot, existing structures, and where proposed work sits relative to property lines and the residence
- **Before context** — current-state photos from consistent angles reviewers can match to the plan
- **Neighboring context** — when sight lines, shared boundaries, or front-elevation visibility matter to the committee

### Proposed work documentation

- **Hardscape layout** — patios, walkways, retaining walls, pool decks, or other paved areas with general dimensions and materials called out
- **Planting plan or planting summary** — species, general placement, and mature size notes where height or screening is a review concern
- **Material palette** — paver, stone, fence, and fixture selections described clearly enough that reviewers know what they are evaluating
- **Elevation or perspective views** — showing how the finished direction reads from the street or from key sight lines the HOA cares about

### Supporting information

- **Project narrative** — a concise written summary of scope, intent, and how the proposal relates to existing architecture
- **Phasing or construction access notes** — if the HOA asks how work will proceed without damaging common areas
- **Reference to applicable HOA guidelines** — citing the design standards section your proposal addresses, when the association provides them

## What a presentation package is — and what it is not

This distinction matters more for HOA work than almost anywhere else, because reviewers and homeowners may treat a polished visual as more authoritative than it is intended to be.

> Human Polish packages are intended for visualization, client presentation, and HOA/design-review support. They do not include architectural, structural, civil, or engineering services; professional seals; permit drawings; construction documents; or code-compliance certification.

An HOA submission-ready presentation package **supports** design-review discussion. It is not:

- Architecture or engineering
- A stamped or sealed drawing set
- Permit drawings, construction documents, or code-compliance certification
- A promise of how an association will rule

**Approval authority remains solely with the reviewing association.** No visualization service — including Human Polish — can promise how a board will rule. Be cautious of any vendor language that implies otherwise.

Use approved framing: "designed to support HOA and design-review submissions" or "HOA submission-ready presentation package" — support language, not outcome guarantees.

## Timeline planning for fixed review cycles

Many HOAs meet on a monthly schedule. Missing a submission window by even a few days can push a client to the next cycle entirely. That is one reason review-before-payment scoping matters: you confirm the package fits your project and timeline **before** you commit, rather than discovering a mismatch after a deadline has passed.

## Pre-submission checklist

Before you send, confirm:

- [ ] You have the HOA's current submission requirements (forms, copies, fees, deadlines)
- [ ] Site and plan materials reflect the survey, photos, and dimensions **you** provided — not assumed conditions
- [ ] Material callouts match what you intend to install, not placeholder selections
- [ ] Views show the proposal on the actual property context, not a generic stand-in
- [ ] Written narrative and visuals tell the same story — no contradictions between plan and render
- [ ] Client understands this is a presentation package for design review, not a permit or construction document
- [ ] You have not represented the package as guaranteeing approval
