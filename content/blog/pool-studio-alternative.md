If you are looking for a Pool Studio alternative, the right choice depends on the job: Pool Studio and Vip3D build full 3D pool designs with dimensioned construction plans, while an AI rendering tool like Renderspace turns a phone photo of the client's yard into a photorealistic concept in about 30 seconds. Many pool builders will want a fast concept to win the sale and a CAD tool for the build.

This post compares the two honestly, so you can decide where each fits in your process.

There's a peculiar problem with designing pools for people who haven't bought one yet: the contractor may need to spend hours creating a polished 3D model just to learn that the homeowner pictured something entirely different. Maybe they wanted a lagoon-style edge, not a rectangle. Maybe the decking should be dark slate rather than pale travertine. Those aren't tiny details to the buyer, but they're difficult to settle by talking over a bare lawn. A quick, adjustable visual helps answer those questions before the full design work begins.

## What do Pool Studio and Vip3D do?

According to [Structure Studios' own FAQ](https://www.structurestudios.com/pool-design-software.md), Pool Studio and Vip3D are 3D design programs for pool builders, landscape designers and outdoor living contractors. They let you:

- Design custom pools, spas, decks, landscaping and terrain in 3D.
- Present with walkthroughs, photorealistic renders and sun simulation, plus VR in Vip3D.
- Produce dimensioned plans, equipment layouts and printable drawings for construction and permits, with cross sections and 2D CAD exports in Vip3D.

They offer a 30-day free trial and subscription licenses, and they run on Windows 10 or 11 with a DirectX 12 graphics card. An optional iPad add-on places a design in a real yard through the camera.

That is a lot of capability. It is also a full design workflow, built for when you are ready to design the pool.

## Should you replace your pool design software?

For most pool builders, no. If you build pools, you need a way to produce real plans. The question is whether you want to spend design hours on every lead before they have said yes.

My line is simple: don't start building a full 3D model while the homeowner is still deciding what they even like. Use a quick concept to narrow the pool shape, spa placement, coping and surrounding patio. Renderspace's custom prompts let you specify details like limestone, slate, a raised spillover spa or sculpted hedges instead of settling for a generic backyard image. Once the homeowner has picked a direction and the project is serious, move into Pool Studio or Vip3D for the measured design.

A simple split many builders can use:

1. **First visit:** photo, fast concept, adjust it with the client in the yard.
2. **Client picks a direction:** build the full design and plans in your CAD tool.
3. **Contract and build:** your normal engineering, permitting and construction process.

## What this looks like on a real job

Imagine a backyard with an existing concrete patio, a wooden privacy fence and a narrow stretch of grass leading toward the rear property line. The homeowner wants a pool and spa but hasn't decided on the shape or surrounding finishes. Building a detailed 3D model immediately would mean committing design time before those preferences are settled.

Instead, the contractor could photograph the yard and use Renderspace to generate an initial concept. The real advantage comes with the next prompts. Perhaps the homeowner wants a freeform pool with light travertine coping, a raised spa with a stone spillway and a cedar pergola beside the decking. Those details can be described directly rather than selected from a handful of generic styles.

If the homeowner prefers darker slate or wants the spa closer to the house, another concept can explore that direction. Once the preferred layout is established, Pool Studio or Vip3D becomes the appropriate place to develop dimensions, elevations, equipment layouts and construction details.
